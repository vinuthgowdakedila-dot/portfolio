The startup I'm building

BUSBOXCloser than you think.
Pre-launch

Parcels that travel with people already making the trip.

BUSBOX is a peer-to-peer, crowd-powered delivery platform. It connects people who need to send a parcel with travellers already heading the same way, so deliveries ride along on journeys that are happening anyway.

Follow BUSBOX (opens in a new tab)

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Sender posts a parcel

    Add the parcel and where it needs to go. Its size and the distance set the price.

  2. Step 2

    A traveller accepts

    Someone already heading in that direction takes on the delivery.

  3. Step 3

    Picked up and delivered

    The traveller collects the parcel and hands it to the recipient at the other end.

The problem

Sending a parcel between cities usually means courier counters, fixed schedules and prices that climb with every kilometre. Meanwhile, people travel those same routes every day with room to spare.

The solution

BUSBOX turns that spare room into delivery capacity. A traveller carries a parcel on a trip they were taking anyway, so the sender pays for a delivery, not a dedicated vehicle — and the longer the route, the stronger the case against a courier.

Two sides of every delivery

How the app is designed to work for each person involved.

  1. Step 1

    Post the parcel

    Size, pickup point and destination.

  2. Step 2

    Get matched

    A traveller heading that way accepts the delivery.

  3. Step 3

    Hand it over

    Meet the traveller and hand over the parcel.

  4. Step 4

    Delivered

    The traveller hands it to the recipient at the destination.

Business model

  • A two-sided marketplace: senders pay per delivery, and travellers are rewarded for carrying parcels on trips they're already taking.
  • Priced by parcel size and distance. The total rises with distance, but the rate per kilometre tapers, because the traveller is going anyway.
  • Long routes are the sweet spot — that's where BUSBOX is designed to undercut courier prices by the widest margin.

How it's built
